Text Splitting & Chunking

Learn about text splitting & chunking.

Why Text Splitting Matters

When building AI agents that work with documents—like a chatbot that answers questions about your company's policies—you can't just feed an entire 100-page manual into the AI at once. Large Language Models (LLMs) have a context window, which is the maximum amount of text they can process in a single request. If your document is too long, the AI will either truncate it (missing important details) or simply refuse to process it.

Text splitting (also called chunking) is the process of breaking large documents into smaller, manageable pieces that fit within the AI's context window. But it's not just about size—it's about meaning. A good chunk should contain a complete thought or idea so the AI can understand it in isolation.

💡 Key Concept: A "chunk" is a segment of text that is small enough to fit in an LLM's context window but large enough to contain meaningful, coherent information.

Common Chunking Strategies

There are several ways to split text, and the best method depends on your use case. Let's explore the most common approaches.

1. Fixed-Size Chunking

The simplest approach: split text every N characters. Easy to implement, but it can break sentences or ideas in half.

from langchain.text_splitter import CharacterTextSplitter

text = "Your long document text goes here. It could be hundreds or thousands of words..."

splitter = CharacterTextSplitter(
    chunk_size=500,      # Each chunk will be ~500 characters
    chunk_overlap=50,    # 50 characters of overlap between chunks
    separator="\n\n"     # Try to split on double newlines first
)

chunks = splitter.split_text(text)
print(f"Created {len(chunks)} chunks")
for i, chunk in enumerate(chunks):
    print(f"Chunk {i+1}: {len(chunk)} characters")
⚠️ Warning: Fixed-size chunking can split a sentence in the middle, which may confuse the AI. Always use chunk_overlap to preserve context between chunks.

2. Recursive Character Splitting

This is the recommended default for most use cases. It tries to split on natural boundaries (paragraphs → sentences → words) before falling back to character count.

from langchain.text_splitter import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter

splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
    chunk_size=1000,
    chunk_overlap=100,
    separators=["\n\n", "\n", ". ", " ", ""]
)

chunks = splitter.split_text(long_document)

3. Semantic Chunking

More advanced: uses embeddings to detect topic shifts and splits where the meaning changes. Best for complex documents but slower and more expensive.

Chunk Size & Overlap: Finding the Sweet Spot

Choosing the right chunk_size and chunk_overlap is crucial. Here's a comparison:

Parameter Too Small Too Large Recommended
chunk_size < 200 chars: Loses context, too fragmented > 2000 chars: May exceed context window, harder to retrieve relevant info 500–1000 chars
chunk_overlap 0: Chunks feel disconnected, AI loses thread > 50% of chunk_size: Wastes tokens, redundant 10–20% of chunk_size
💡 Pro Tip: Start with chunk_size=800 and chunk_overlap=80. Adjust based on your specific documents and how well your AI retrieves relevant chunks.

Hands-On: Splitting a Real Document

Let's put this into practice. Imagine you have a product manual and want to prepare it for an AI agent.

from langchain_community.document_loaders import TextLoader
from langchain.text_splitter import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter

# Step 1: Load the document
loader = TextLoader("product_manual.txt")
documents = loader.load()

# Step 2: Split into chunks
splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
    chunk_size=750,
    chunk_overlap=75,
    length_function=len,
    add_start_index=True  # Track where each chunk starts in the original doc
)

chunks = splitter.split_documents(documents)

# Step 3: Inspect the results
print(f"Original document: {len(documents[0].page_content)} characters")
print(f"Number of chunks: {len(chunks)}")
print(f"\nFirst chunk preview:\n{chunks[0].page_content[:200]}...")
print(f"\nMetadata: {chunks[0].metadata}")

Exercise: Build Your Own Chunker

Exercise

You have a list of customer reviews (strings). Write a function that:

  1. Combines all reviews into one string
  2. Splits it into chunks of max 300 characters with 30-character overlap
  3. Returns a list of chunks

Use RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter from LangChain.

from langchain.text_splitter import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter

def chunk_reviews(reviews: list[str]) -> list[str]:
    combined = "\n\n".join(reviews)
    
    splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
        chunk_size=300,
        chunk_overlap=30,
        separators=["\n\n", "\n", ". ", " ", ""]
    )
    
    return splitter.split_text(combined)

# Example usage
reviews = [
    "Great product! Works exactly as described.",
    "Shipping was fast but packaging could be better...",
    # ... more reviews
]
chunks = chunk_reviews(reviews)
print(f"Created {len(chunks)} chunks from {len(reviews)} reviews")

Key Takeaways

  • Chunking is essential for fitting large documents into an LLM's context window.
  • RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter is the best general-purpose splitter—it respects natural text boundaries.
  • chunk_overlap (10–20% of chunk size) prevents context loss between chunks.
  • chunk_size of 500–1000 characters works well for most use cases.
  • Always inspect your chunks after splitting to ensure they contain coherent ideas.
  • Different documents (code, legal text, chat logs) may need custom splitting strategies.
